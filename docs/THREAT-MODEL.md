# What each party can do (1.0)

Who can do what to a VoIPC user, as of 1.0, and what in the code holds each
"cannot". Tests are named so they can be found with a search; `test-ui` checks
run in the browser lanes of `test-web.sh`.

| Who | Can | Cannot | Held by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The relay, honest or only curious** | See metadata: who is connected and from which address, which channel, when, and how big each packet is. With accounts, also what [ACCOUNTS.md](ACCOUNTS.md) lists for kept messages: who wrote where, when, how long, and each copy's timer. | Read messages, voice, video, screen audio, positions, kept copies or vault items. | libsignal sessions; `media_keys` (AES-256-GCM); `log::seal_entry`; the vault (`account::Vault`). |
| **The relay, actively hostile, for desktop users** | Meet guests in the middle: a guest's identity is per connection and not verified, by design. Seat a listener in a channel (the member list shows it). With accounts: meet you before the person you meant to meet; hide, drop or roll back vault items, the pins included; withhold kept copies or hand them out again; guess a password offline, at 600,000 PBKDF2-SHA256 rounds a guess; keep a writer on an old key of their own, which helps it only together with a member who left. | Read kept copies or vault items without the password. Forge or alter a member's kept entry (each is signed). Add a reader without the writer's device naming them. Replace a pinned key unnoticed, unless it removes the pins first, and then a device that saw them warns. Make one member's voice play as another's. Bring back entries already read, or a cleared conversation. Claim another relay's id to get a password stretched for that one. | `a_rewritten_sender_fails`; `a_replayed_session_start_on_the_last_resort_key_is_refused`; `vault_pins_it_cannot_trust_are_reported_and_never_saved_over`; `a_merged_pin_that_disagrees_is_reported`; `a_server_claiming_another_servers_relay_id_gets_nothing_of_the_password`; `AccountSync::drain` drops entries at or below the read cursor; `the_recovery_key_sets_a_new_password_and_signs_every_device_out` (the relay checks the identity's signature). |
| **The relay, actively hostile, for browser users** | Everything above, and serve a page that reads what you type (your password included) and takes the remembered device keys and vault keys this page keeps, for every server opened in it. | Nothing the page itself cannot do. | Only the desktop app; the connect dialog warns when an account is on another server than the page's own. test-ui: "a server claiming another's relay id is refused". |
| **A member of a channel** | Read what is said there while a member, and keep copies. Hand over history, which is shown as second-hand. Prove that you wrote a kept message (they are signed). | Forge another member's message or kept entry. Read what is written after they left a text channel (the relay takes them out of its log, and the next message is under a new key) or after the next media key once they left a voice channel. Offer a media key for a room they are not in. Speak as another member. | `leaving_a_text_channel_ends_its_readership`; the media-key rules in `channel_state` and `media_keys`; `a_rewritten_sender_fails`. |
| **Someone with the device's disk** | Read the desktop's `settings.json` (servers, names, relay ids) and `tofu_pins.json` (which servers were used), if they can read your user's files. Guess the history password offline (600,000 PBKDF2-SHA256 rounds a guess). In the browser, read localStorage (read positions, relay ids); with the browser profile and its live session, use the remembered device keys. | Open the history file without its password. Read the desktop's files as another user on the machine (folder 0700, files 0600 on Linux and Android). Copy the browser's wrapping key to another profile. | `crypto.rs` (archive v5); `config::write_atomic` (0600); `data_dir` (0700); `devices.ts` (non-extractable AES key). |
| **A hostile page from another site** | Nothing of VoIPC's storage. | Read the relay origin's IndexedDB or localStorage. | The browser's same-origin rule. |

What changed for 1.0: post-quantum sessions, the sender in the media AAD, pre-key
refill, rolled-back or missing vault items reported instead of reset, a known
account shown as a guest reported, cleared direct conversations kept cleared,
the relay-id check in the browser, and readership ending when you leave a text
channel.
